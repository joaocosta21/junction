## Assumptions
- The Interactions API, not `generateContent`; `store=False`; thinking level `low`.
- The schema sent to the model is written by hand to follow the contract, not the contract's own JSON Schema: that one carries keywords the API ignores or may refuse as too complex (`maxItems` 1000, patterns) and cannot say "not stated". A test compares its fields with `PurchaseRequest` and `RequestLine`.
- In the model's answer every field is required and `null` means "the text does not say".
- `text` of every line is the whole pasted text, set by the parser, never by the model.
- What the text does not say (no line, no product, quantity, unit, allergen strictness) is `invalid-request` at once, with `missing`; the model is not asked again.
- An answer that does not validate is asked for once more, then `invalid-request` with `faults`: field paths and fixed reasons, never the model's words.
- Beyond the schema the parser refuses: a product type outside the table, a claim outside the catalog's 21, an allergen code outside `tta.allergens` or `X99`, a product id that is not in the text, a date before the clock's date.
- "By Monday" is resolved by the model from a calendar of the next 14 days placed in the instruction, with the rule "the first such day after today" (question 3).
- With a product type, `name_terms` only narrow it ("oat drink" is `plant_drink` plus `oat`); the gateway applies both.
- The strictness a profile sets is read from the policy files by a second `RulePolicyEngine.from_files()`: the hook's signature has no policy.
- The loop offers four functions; three take no arguments and `place_order` takes `option_id` only. The offer is built for the request the parser read; an order names an option of the run's latest offer; one run places one order (question 4).
- "Pick the top option" is an instruction to the model, not enforced: it may order any option of the offer, and the gateway's verdict applies to whichever it picks.
- A step is one gateway call. Reading the text is not a step; the typed request shows as the arguments of the `create_offer` step.
- The report is the ordered option's summary, the policy decision's sentence and the order's status sentence. With no order: the offer's sentence or notes, the last refusal, "No order was placed." The model's closing words are discarded.
- A gateway refusal is a failed step handed back to the model, not the end of the run.
- When the model cannot be reached after at least one step, the run is returned and its report says so; before any step it is `supplier-unavailable`.
- One run is at most 10 model requests before retries (2 answers for the reading, 8 turns).
- A recorded run holds function names and arguments per turn, no ids. Past its last turn the recorded model ends the run.
- The hand-built readings say Monday 16 Nov 2026: right while the data clock is between 9 and 15 Nov 2026, as both captures are; after 16 Nov the parser refuses them as before today.
- A fixture file that does not validate, or two for one sentence, stops the start.
- Errors and log lines of the Gemini module carry the HTTP status only; the SDK's error is not chained.
- In the trace, a refused call keeps at most 5 arguments of 60 characters, and a function name 64.
- The idempotency key of an order is `agent-run:<run id>:<digest of offer and option>`.

## Contract changes needed
- `AgentRun.model`: the comment names the model id or `recorded`; a third value `hand-built` is now used (question 2).
- `AgentRun`: no field for why a run ended. `stopped_at_cap` covers one reason; "the model stopped answering" is only a sentence in the report.
- `AgentRun`: no field for the typed request; it is inside the `create_offer` step's arguments.
- The hooks: `build_request_parser(..., policy=...)` would spare the second read of the policy files.
- `InvalidRequest`: the extension members `missing` and `faults` are not described anywhere in the contracts.
- `Settings`: no field for the model's time-out, retries or thinking level; they are constants in `gemini.py`.
