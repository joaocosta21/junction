# T08 — questions

All four are for the team, not for Valio.

**Context:** SPEC §8 shows `approve ─► re-check ─► ORDERED` and does not say what happens to a waiting order when that re-check fails. **We did:** on a failed re-check the approver gets `offer-stale` (or `offer-expired`), one `order.not_placed` event is written in the order's trail, and nothing else changes: the order stays `PENDING_APPROVAL` and no decision is recorded. **We got:** the approver can still approve another option of the same offer or reject; if nobody does, the order becomes `EXPIRED` at its "order by" time. Until then it still counts in the day's spend. **Example to show:** `tests/orders/test_decide.py`, `test_a_price_that_changed_before_the_approval_is_offer_stale_and_the_order_keeps_waiting`. **Ask:** "A price changes while an order waits; the approver sees `offer-stale` and the order keeps waiting until its time runs out — is that expected, or did we get something wrong (should it close at once)?" **If no answer, we build:** as described.

**Context:** an approver may pick another option of the same offer (SPEC §8). With two roles required, one human may already have approved the first option. **We did:** choosing another option starts the approval again on that option: only the decision of the human who chose it is kept on the approval, and the roles are the ones the policy asks for on the new option. Earlier approvals stay in the audit trail. **We got:** finance approves option 1 (€3,180.00); the purchasing manager then picks option 2 (€3,445.00); the order waits for finance again. **Example to show:** `tests/orders/test_decide.py`, `test_choosing_another_option_starts_the_approval_again_on_that_option`. **Ask:** "An approval given for one option does not carry over to another option — is that expected, or did we get something wrong?" **If no answer, we build:** as described.

**Context:** every refused attempt to decide an approval writes `authorisation.refused` (orchestrator's change 4). SPEC §9 says a buyer does not see other buyers. **We did:** when the approval exists, the event is filed with it, so it appears in the trail of the order it was aimed at, with the caller's id and display name. **We got:** the hotel's trail reads "Dietitian (demo) (dietitian) was refused on approval apr_…: this caller may not decide it. This approver belongs to another buyer." — the hotel learns the id and name of the ward's approver who tried. A refused supplier reader is recorded as a human with role `supplier`, and the supplier view shows it as "A human at the buyer". **Example to show:** `tests/orders/test_decide.py`, `test_a_caller_who_may_not_decide_is_refused_and_the_attempt_is_recorded`. **Ask:** "The buyer whose approval was targeted sees who tried, another buyer's approver included — is that expected, or did we get something wrong?" **If no answer, we build:** as described (one line in `OrderBookService._refused` files the event under the caller's own buyer instead).

**Context:** SPEC §9 lists "policy evaluated" as a step; the task asks for "one audit event" on a blocked order. **We did:** the order service writes one event per outcome and no separate `policy.evaluated`: `order.placed_by_agent`, `approval.requested`, `order.blocked` and `order.not_placed` each carry the order-time decision (rule ids, the facts read, the policy hash) and the fresh check. **We got:** an order placed by the agent has one event from this package; an approved one has `approval.requested`, `approval.decided`, `order.confirmed`. `policy.evaluated` appears in a trail only if the offer service (T09) writes it when the offer is built. **Example to show:** `tests/orders/test_audit_trail.py`, `test_no_status_changes_without_an_audit_event_in_the_same_call`. **Ask:** "At order time the policy's answer is inside the outcome event, with no `policy.evaluated` row of its own — is that expected, or did we get something wrong?" **If no answer, we build:** as described (a separate row is one `record` call in `OrderTimeChecker.check`).
