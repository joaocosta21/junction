# T02 — questions

## 1. For Valio — is an `allowedLotSize` of 0.01 "no step"?
**Context:** `allowedLotSize` is 0.01 on 16,872 of the 17,546 products (catalog). That covers 16,476 of the 16,478 products that are not sold by the kilogram: cartons, cases, bags, bottles. A real step (1.5, 3.0 …) is on 674 products, 672 of them sold by the kilogram.
**We did:** rounded every quantity up to the product's step, as the field describes.
**We got:** for a countable sales unit a 0.01 step allows a fraction of a case. In our three captures (a sample of 1,745 product ids) no `availableAmount` of any unit has a fraction.
**Example to show:** `463038`, "Valio Eila semi skimmed milk drink 10 l novobox lactose free", sales unit `KI` (a case of two 10-litre boxes), `allowedLotSize` 0.01. 250 litres is 12.5 cases; a 0.01 step would let us order 12.5 cases.
**Ask:** "We read 0.01 as 'no step set' and order whole cartons and cases, so 13 cases here. Is that expected, or did we get something wrong?"
**If no answer, we build:** whole units for every sales unit except `KG`; a product sold by the kilogram with 0.01 keeps 0.01 kg steps; a real step is used as given.

## 2. For Valio — what does `comparisonPrice` divide by?
**Context:** no field states the litres or kilograms in one sales unit. We derive it as `catalogPrice ÷ comparisonPrice`.
**We did:** compared that ratio with what the product file says (the `unitConversions` row, else the size in the name) on the 1,321 captured price lines that have a comparison price and are not sold by the kilogram (a sample).
**We got:** the ratio is rounded, because both prices have two decimals: 8.75 ÷ 0.88 = 9.943 for a 10-litre box. The file's figure fits inside the half-cent rounding of the comparison price on 186 of 190 lines (litres, conversion row) and 481 of 513 (kg, conversion row). Where it does not fit, the comparison price seems to use another weight than `netWeight`.
**Example to show:** `460396`, "Valio skimmed milk 10 l novobox": 8.75 ÷ 0.88 = 9.943, file says 10 litres. `466382`, "Filos feta cubes PDO in brine 2kg": `netWeight` 3.39 kg, price ratio 2.0.
**Ask:** "We take `comparisonPrice` to be `catalogPrice` divided by the content of one sales unit (the drained weight where there is one), rounded to cents. Is that expected, or did we get something wrong?"
**If no answer, we build:** when the product file's own figure is consistent with the two prices we use that figure (10 litres, not 9.943); otherwise we use the ratio. Either way the offer says the pack size came from the price.

## 3. For the team — is 50 g the right limit for a portion pack?
**Context:** SPEC §3 step 6 excludes single-serve packs from a `BULK` line: an inner unit of 5 cl or 50 g or less.
**We did:** applied that limit to every product whose sales unit holds more than one inner unit.
**We got:** 744 of 17,546 products are portion packs by this rule (catalog). `463203` (100 × 2 cl) is one, as intended. So are cases of small retail packs and of small pieces.
**Example to show:** `468798` "6x Meira Flavoury black peppercorn mill 49g" and `465619` "Findus Fishfinger MSC ca 360x28g" are both portion packs under the rule. A `BULK` request for pepper or fish fingers in kilograms would exclude them with `PORTION_PACK`.
**Ask:** "Do we keep the 50 g limit as it is, lower it, or apply the rule only to drinks and spreads? Is that outcome expected, or did we get something wrong?"
**If no answer, we build:** the SPEC's limit as written. The exclusion is shown with its reason, and `pack: ANY` on the request line turns the rule off.
