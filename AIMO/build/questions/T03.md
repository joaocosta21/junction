# T03 — questions (2 for Valio, 1 for the team)

All counts are from the whole catalog (17,546 products), read from the product file.

## Q1 — For Valio: `471881` is named "gluten free" and declares cereals containing gluten

**Context:** the substitution check. A substitute must not declare an allergen that the original does not declare. `473609` (Oddlygood Barista oat drink 1 l UHT gluten free) lists three substitutes.
**We did:** compared the `allergen` group of `473609` with the `allergen` group of each listed substitute.
**We got:** `473609` and `471690` declare only `GO` (oats) `CONTAINS`. `471881` declares `AW` `CONTAINS` and `GO` `CONTAINS`, although its name says "gluten free" and it carries the claim `FREE_FROM_GLUTEN`. `464320` (Oatly iKaffe 1L) declares `AW` and `GO` as well.
**Example to show:**

| Product | Name | `allergen` group | Claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| `473609` | Oddlygood® Barista oat drink 1 l UHT gluten free | `GO` CONTAINS | `FREE_FROM_GLUTEN` |
| `471690` | Oddlygood® Barista oat drink 1 L Delight UHT gluten free | `GO` CONTAINS | `FREE_FROM_GLUTEN` |
| `471881` | Oddlygood® Barista oat drink 0,5 l UHT gluten free | **`AW` CONTAINS**, `GO` CONTAINS | `FREE_FROM_GLUTEN` |

**Ask:** "The 0.5 l pack of the same gluten-free oat drink declares cereals containing gluten and the 1 l packs do not. Is that expected, or did we get something wrong?"
**If no answer, we build:** the allergen declaration counts, never the name or the claim. `471881` fails the substitution rule for `473609` (it adds `AW`) and cannot be ordered as its substitute.

## Q2 — For Valio: a family stated `FREE_FROM` while one of its members is declared

**Context:** a buyer excludes a family code, for example `AW` (cereals containing gluten) or `AN` (tree nuts). We also read the statements on the member codes (wheat, oats, almonds and so on).
**We did:** counted products whose `nonAllergen` group holds the family code while the `allergen` group declares (`CONTAINS` or `MAY_CONTAIN`) a member of that family.
**We got:** 178 products: 132 for `AW` (76 of them with oats `GO`, 35 with wheat `UW` `CONTAINS`), 45 for `AN` (19 with a member `CONTAINS`), 1 for `AM` with lactose `ML`.
**Example to show:**

| Product | Name | Family statement | Member statement |
|---|---|---|---|
| `460124` | Fria Fiber-rich loaf 500g x 8 glutenfree frozen sliced | `AW` FREE_FROM | `UW` (wheat) CONTAINS |
| `462810` | Atria Cabbage Rolls 2kg/87g | `AW` FREE_FROM | `GO` (oats) CONTAINS |
| `461333` | 20 X Toblerone countline 100g | `AN` FREE_FROM | `SA` (almonds) CONTAINS |

**Ask:** "We read 'free from cereals containing gluten' together with 'contains wheat' as gluten-free wheat starch, and 'free from tree nuts' together with 'contains almonds' as an error in the data. Is that expected, or did we get something wrong?"
**If no answer, we build:** the family statement and the member statement are two statements and the least safe one counts. With the family excluded, a member `CONTAINS` rejects the product (oats go to a dietitian, SPEC §6). A family `FREE_FROM` is never read as a statement about a member that has no statement of its own.

## Q3 — For the team: gluten-free wheat starch under a "no gluten" exclusion

**Context:** SPEC §6 sends declared oats to a dietitian when `AW` is excluded, because gluten-free oats exist. The catalog shows the same pattern for wheat.
**We did:** ran the exclusion rule with `AW` excluded over the catalog.
**We got:** 35 products state `AW` `FREE_FROM` and `UW` `CONTAINS`. The rule rejects them (conflicting statements, the least safe counts). 243 products go to a dietitian through the oat rule.
**Example to show:** `460124` Fria Fiber-rich loaf, "glutenfree", claim `FREE_FROM_GLUTEN`, `AW` FREE_FROM, `UW` CONTAINS → `REJECT` for a ward that excludes `AW`.
**Ask:** "Should a product that is free from gluten cereals as a family and contains wheat go to a dietitian like oats, instead of being rejected? Is the rejection expected, or did we get something wrong?"
**If no answer, we build:** `REJECT`, as SPEC §6 says for conflicting statements. Only oats have the exception.
