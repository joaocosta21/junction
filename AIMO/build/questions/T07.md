# T07 — questions

## Q1 — for the team: may the agent order alone a substitute that drops a nutritional claim of the original?

**Context:** The substitution rule of SPEC §6 looks at declared allergens only. A nutritional claim is not an allergen statement: a lactose-free milk and a regular milk both declare milk (`AM`).

**We did:** ranked the substitutes of the lactose-free milk `469363` (replay, tonight's capture, hand-built stock).

**We got:** 32 regular milks of category 10400 (catalog) pass the substitution rule for `469363`: they add no allergen. One of them is offered as the stand-in when `469363` is named by id and cannot be delivered, and `hotel-kitchen` (`SAME_CATEGORY`) gets verdict `AUTO` for it.

**Example to show:** hand-built availability (not tonight's capture): 100 L of `469363` named by id, not deliverable; `463149` Valio Plus skimmed milk 1 l (no `FREE_FROM_LACTOSE`) with 500 confirmed at €1.10 → one option, `SUBSTITUTE`, 100 × `463149`, €110.00, verdict `AUTO`. Its `gives_up` says: "it does not carry the original's nutritional claim FREE_FROM_LACTOSE".

**Ask:** "Should a substitute that loses a claim of the original (lactose-free, gluten-free) need a human, or be refused? Today only the sentence says it — is that expected, or did we get something wrong?"

**If no answer, we build:** as built: `gives_up` names every lost claim; a stand-in in an offer must carry the claims the request line lists in `required_claims`; nothing else stops it. Proposed: `SubstituteInfo.claims_lost` and a policy rule that sends such a line to a human.

## Q2 — for the team: a substitute when the named product is only "quantity not confirmed"?

**Context:** SPEC §4 says blocked, not deliverable and out of stock trigger substitution for a product asked for by id. SPEC §5 says `SUBSTITUTE` exists when the named products "cannot be supplied in full".

**We did:** built the `SUBSTITUTE` option whenever confirmed stock of the named products leaves something missing, so also when the named product is `QUANTITY_NOT_CONFIRMED` or past its cut-off.

**We got:** `473609` named by id, 100 L, `hotel-kitchen`, tonight: option 1 `SUBSTITUTE` 100 × `475224` (€174.00, verdict `AUTO`), option 2 `ALTERNATIVE_UNCONFIRMED` 100 × `473609` (€137.00, `APPROVAL` by F-01). The stand-in is ranked first because a quantity that is not confirmed is never the suggestion.

**Example to show:** the two options above.

**Ask:** "When the product asked for by id is available but its quantity is not confirmed, should the agent be offered (and allowed to order alone) a confirmed stand-in first — is that expected, or did we get something wrong?"

**If no answer, we build:** as built.

## Q3 — for the team: a partial fill of the product asked for, or a full fill with a stand-in, first?

**Context:** SPEC §5 ranks options by constraint fit, then verification status, then price.

**We did:** hand-built availability: `469363` named by id, 100 L, 40 confirmed; `462775` has 500.

**We got:** option 1 `BEST_CONFIRMED` 40 L of `469363` (60 L missing, `APPROVAL` by P-03); option 2 `SUBSTITUTE` 40 L of `469363` plus 60 L of `462775` (full, `AUTO` for `hotel-kitchen`). Each misses one thing the buyer asked (the quantity; the product), so the tie is broken by kind: the product asked for comes first.

**Example to show:** the two options above.

**Ask:** "The buyer's agent picks option 1. Should the full fill with a stand-in come first instead — is the order we chose expected, or did we get something wrong?"

**If no answer, we build:** as built: the product asked for first.

## Q4 — for the team: is stock that moved but still covers the order a change?

**Context:** `revalidate` runs before every order. F-04 blocks the order when it reports any change.

**We did:** reported a change of stock only when the fresh figure no longer covers what the option orders of that product.

**We got:** 384 offered, 116 ordered, stock now 120 → no change, the order goes ahead. Stock now 100 → `available_amount` changed, `offer-stale`.

**Example to show:** `tests/offers/test_revalidate.py`, the two stock tests.

**Ask:** "Stock moves with every other buyer's order. We treat 'enough for this order' as the statement the offer relied on, not the exact figure — is that expected, or did we get something wrong?"

**If no answer, we build:** as built. A price that differs in either direction, a new cut-off and a new price validity are always changes.

**Context:** The product file lists substitutes for 189 products. We convert quantities through litres or kilograms per sales unit.

**We did:** looked up the substitutes of `473609` (Oddlygood Barista oat drink 1 l, sold by the piece, €1.37).

**We got:** the listed substitute `471690` (Oddlygood Barista oat drink 1 L Delight) is sold by the case of 10 litres at €15.55, so 100 pieces of the original become 10 cases. Tonight its order cut-off has also passed (Thursday 12 Nov 15:00), so it cannot be ordered.

**Example to show:** `POST /v1/products/473609/substitutes` → rank 1 `471690`, "one sales unit holds 10 L, not 1 L; it costs €0.19 more per L", not orderable: cut-off passed.

**Ask:** "When a listed substitute is sold in another sales unit than the original, do you expect the buyer to convert the quantity by volume, as we do — is that expected, or did we get something wrong?"

**If no answer, we build:** as built: quantities are converted through the pack size and rounded up to the lot step.
