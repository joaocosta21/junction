**The protocols today (read today unless marked).**
- **x402.** "x402 is the open payment standard that enables services to charge for access to their APIs and content directly over HTTP." Flow: "A buyer requests a resource from a server."; "If payment is required, the server responds with `402 Payment Required`, including payment instructions."; "The buyer prepares and submits a payment payload."; the server verifies and settles, itself or through "an x402 facilitator's /verify and /settle endpoints"; "If payment is valid, the server provides the requested resource." (https://docs.x402.org/). In version 2 the terms travel in a `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` header, the proof in `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE`, the result in `PAYMENT-RESPONSE`; schemes are "exact, upto, and batch-settlement" (https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402). The terms are a list `accepts` of `scheme`, `network` (for example `eip155:8453`, "Base mainnet"), `amount` "in atomic token units", `asset` ("Token contract address or ISO 4217 currency code for fiat"), `payTo`, `maxTimeoutSeconds`. The proof, for the `exact` scheme on EVM chains, is an "EIP-712 signature for authorization" over "EIP-3009 authorization parameters" (`from`, `to`, `value`, `validAfter`, `validBefore`, `nonce`). The result carries a "Blockchain transaction hash". "Non-blockchain networks are encouraged to follow the CAIP-2 format (e.g., `ach:us`, `sepa:eu`)." (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/x402-foundation/x402/main/specs/x402-specification-v2.md). Backing: the Linux Foundation announced on 14 July 2026 the x402 Foundation, "a new open-governance body formed to steward the x402 protocol", after "the completed contribution of the x402 protocol by Coinbase"; premier members include Adyen, AWS, American Express, Circle, Cloudflare, Coinbase, Fiserv, Google, Mastercard, Shopify, Stripe and Visa; it speaks of "payment types ranging from traditional cards to stablecoins" (https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-announces-operational-launch-of-x402-foundation-to-standardize-internet-native-payments-for-ai-agents-and-applications). Rails today: "Supports stablecoins & tokens across Base, Solana, other chains", and "Facilitators for ACH, SEPA, or card networks fit into the same payment model." (https://www.x402.org/writing/x402-v2-launch, last updated 2026-06-24); the project also says "x402 may extend support to fiat based networks, but will never deprioritize onchain payments in favor of fiat payments" (GitHub page above). Status: an Apache-2.0 specification at version 2 under a foundation; not a standards-body document.
- **Nevermined.** A company's product, not a standard: "Nevermined - Payments Infrastructure for AI Agents". To a buyer's agent: "Your agents spend inside a mandate you set"; a spend mandate is "The limit you set before an agent can pay: an amount, an expiry and a number of purchases." To a seller: "Get listed in the catalog so agents can find you."; "Agents pay per call over x402 or MPP, and you serve once the payment checks out."; "Nevermined Pay settles the merchant on the rail they take: cards, Visa agentic tokens or crypto." (https://nevermined.ai/). Relation to x402: it runs a facilitator, "an enforcement and settlement engine for HTTP-native agent payments", and adds its own x402 schemes, `nvm:erc4337` and `nvm:card-delegation`, with plans by "credits, subscriptions, PAYG, dynamic charging" (https://nevermined.ai/docs/products/x402-facilitator/overview). MPP is the Machine Payments Protocol, "the open standard for machine-to-machine payments via HTTP 402", "co-authored by Tempo and Stripe" (https://mpp.dev/).
- **Agent Payments Protocol (AP2).** "an open protocol for the emerging Agent Economy", an extension for A2A and UCP; the page links "AP2 v0.2 Release and FIDO Alliance Donation". Two mandates, each open then closed. Checkout Mandate, open: "Captures the user's constraints and goals for the transaction before a specific cart is finalized for autonomous execution."; closed: "Captures the user's (or agent's) authorization for a specific, finalized checkout."; "shared with the merchant". Payment Mandate, closed: "Captures the authorization for a specific transaction amount bound to a finalized checkout."; shared "with the Credential Provider, Networks and the Merchant Payment Processor". Mandates are "tamper-evident, cryptographically signed digital objects". First version: "common 'pull' payment methods like credit and debit cards" (https://ap2-protocol.org/). A closed checkout mandate holds `checkout_jwt`, "the merchant-signed JWT containing the details of the checkout" (https://ap2-protocol.org/ap2/checkout_mandate/). The older names "Intent Mandate" and "Cart Mandate" are not on the current page; a judge may still use them.
- **Agentic Commerce Protocol.** "an interaction model and open standard for connecting buyers, their AI agents, and businesses to complete purchases"; "maintained by OpenAI and Stripe"; "currently in beta", latest stable version 2026-04-17; a checkout specification and a "Delegate Payment Spec". Its page has no word on invoices, payment terms or purchase orders (https://github.com/agentic-commerce-protocol/agentic-commerce-protocol).
- **Card networks.** Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol is "a verifiable, cryptographic standard to help merchants differentiate legitimate agents": "a trusted agent will include a message signature in its request to your server" (RFC 9421). It identifies an agent; the page does not say it moves money (https://developer.visa.com/capabilities/trusted-agent-protocol/overview). Mastercard: Agent Pay with "Agentic Tokens", and "Verifiable Intent" (5 March 2026, with Google, aligned with AP2 and UCP): **search result only**, the page refused my request (https://www.mastercard.com/global/en/news-and-trends/stories/2026/verifiable-intent.html).

## 3. What Valio's answers change
- **V8, the user always confirms.** The confirmation becomes the one authorisation event of every order. That is the part of the payment standards worth mapping: AP2's closed checkout mandate is a person's authorisation of one finalised checkout. A protocol where the agent pays alone inside a request (402, pay, retry) goes the other way, on the day Valio asked us to switch off ordering alone.
- **Q16, "what is actually delivered".** At order time the amount is an estimate for every line sold by weight (632 catalog products, `AIMO/PLAN.md:225`). A payment taken at order time would have to be corrected after delivery. x402's `upto` scheme is the nearest idea ("authorizes a transfer of up to a **maximum amount**"), but "The settled `amount` MUST be `<=` the authorized maximum" (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/x402-foundation/x402/main/specs/schemes/upto/scheme_upto.md), and a delivered weight can be above the ordered weight.
- **V5, ask when unsure.** How a customer pays is account data the mock does not have. We state our assumption (credit customer) and ask Valio (§8). We do not invent a credit limit and enforce it.
- **The goal, "attract as many customers as possible"** (`BRIEF.md:9`). x402's home ground is charging per call for an API. Charging an agent to read Valio's prices works against that goal. Not proposed.
